## Trade-off analysis

### Immediate

Advantages:
- No cases missed at all with the the least amount of delay in identifying a case
- From sample of 20 plots chosen, for immediate policy, majority of cases identified were cases worth investigating (68.8%)
- From controlled tests conducted, immediate was able to identify all patterns with no delay

Disadvantages:
- Workload is high: number of cases identified per monitored week on an average is about ten times that of other policies, the most out of all the policies
- From the sample of 20 plots chosen, for immediate policy, there is still a significant number of cases identified which are not worth investigating (only to be monitored)

Trade-off analysis:
Choosing immediate policy would mean that cases worth investigating are never missed 100% of the time, but that also means more noisy cases, where the facilities manager could be alerted of a case even when it is something not worth investigating.

### Continuous20

Advantages:
- Average delay in identifying a case is the second lowest out of the four policies
- From the sample of 20 plots chosen, 100% of the cases identified by the continuous20 policy are deemed to be worth investigating, with 0% of them being cases that only need to be monitored.
- From controlled tests conducted, all cases that were continuous for 20 mins or more were identified

Disadvantages:
- Cases missed is significantly high (91.2%)
- Average delay in identifying a case is still significantly high despite being the second lowest out of the four policies
- From controlled tests conducted, number of different patterns of cases continuous20 policy was able to identify is significantly low (only 4 out of 12)
  - Unable to identify short spikes and cases that last only for continuous 10 minutes
  - Unable to identify intermittent cases
- From controlled tests conducted, for the patterns that continuous20 managed to identify, there is a delay of 19 minutes before the case is identified

Trade-off analysis:
Choosing continuous20 would mean significantly lesser workload than immediate(lesser number of cases). However, this significant decrease in workload also means more cases are missed, which causes the policy to be unable to identify cases of certain patterns(cannot identify brief cases that last for less than 20 consecutive minutes). However, despite being able to identify less cases, each of the case identified is highly accurate and worth investigating (as shown through the 100% investigation rate in the test with the 20 chosen plots)

### Continuous30

Advantages:
- Number of cases per monitored week is the lowest out of all the four policies
- From the sample of 20 plots chosen, 100% of the cases identified by the continuous30 policy are deemed to be worth investigating, with 0% of them being cases that only need to be monitored.
- From controlled tests conducted, all cases that were continuous for 30 mins or more were identified

Disadvantages:
- Cases missed is the highest out of the four policies (94.1%)
- Average delay in identifying a case is the highest out of the four policies
- From controlled tests conducted, number of different patterns of cases continuous30 policy was able to identify is the lowest (only 2 out of 12)
  - Unable to identify short spikes and cases that last only for less than continuous 30 minutes
  - Unable to identify intermittent cases
- From controlled tests conducted, for the patterns that continuous30 managed to identify, there is a delay of 29 mins before the case is identified

Trade-off analysis:
Choosing continuous30 would mean having the least amount of workload of the four policies(least number of cases), but that would also come at the expense of missing the most number of cases and being able to identify the least number of different types of patterns. Whilst the accuracy of cases identified is high(100% investigation rate), there is a large number of cases missed out, possibly leaving out a large number of cases worth investigating for the sake of a smaller workload.

### 20of30

Advantages:
- Second lowest number of missed cases of the four policies (89.6%)
- From the sample of 20 plots chosen, majority of the cases identified are worth investigating(83.3% of cases identified)
- From controlled tests conducted, all cases that were continuous for 20 minutes or more were identified, as well as some of the intermittent cases.
- From controlled tests conducted, 20of30 policy was able to identify most of the patterns (7 out of 12) which is already the next highest after immediate policy

Disadvantages:
- Average delay for identifying a case is the second highest, with the total delay is the highest out of the four policies
- Number of missed cases are still significantly high despite being the second lowest of the four policies
- From the sample of 20 plots chosen, as compared to that for continuous20 and continuous30, 20of30 policy is not as accurate since there are still cases identified that are not worth investigating (to be monitored only)
- From controlled tests conducted, same like continuous20 and continuous30 policies, 20of30 policy also cannot identify cases of short spikes
  - Also unable to identify cases of continuous abnormal reading of 10 mins only, and some of the intermittent cases
- From controlled tests conducted, for the patterns that 20of30 managed to identify, there is an unfixed delay before the case is identified

Trade-off analysis:
Choosing the 20of30 policy would serve as a good middle ground between the immediate policy and continuous policies, having the flexibility to identify both continuous and intermittent cases whilst still significantly reducing the workload as compared to immediate. At the same time, 20of30 is able to have a higher accuracy than immediate, having a higher percentage of cases identified worth an investigation by the facilities manager. However, the number of missed cases are still significantly high.

## Missed-pattern severity

This is calculated on the full data (all 623 cases). All three persistence policies miss every immediate_only case. Continuous20 and Continuous30 also miss the interruption_tolerance_adds_case cases, and Continuous30 also misses the shorter_duration_adds_case cases. No persistence policy misses a shared_sustained_case. Findings in step 1 is used as the estimate for that group's missed cases:

| review_group | Plots | Rated investigate | Investigate share (Rated Investigate/Plots) |
|---|---|---|---|
| immediate_only | 4 | 1 (R19) | 0.25 |
| interruption_tolerance_adds_case | 4 | 2 (R01, R04) | 0.50 |
| shorter_duration_adds_case | 4 | 4 | 1.00 |
| shared_sustained_case | 4 | 4 | 1.00 |

### Calculated estimate

Estimated missed cases worth investigating = for each review_group, missed cases in the group × the group's investigate share, added up.

Looking at ‘estimated worth investigating’, 20of30 has the least severe misses, then Continuous20, then Continuous30, in both periods.
